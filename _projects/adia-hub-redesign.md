## The problem

The Adia Hub is the core product of Adia Thermal: a pipework assembly with a pump, sensors and a Raspberry Pi/PCBA
controller that communicates with smart radiator valves and temperature sensors. The pilot version cost £1,072 per unit,
so every sale lost money. It was also hard to assemble at scale, relied on a weak supply chain and was complicated to install.

## My role

I was the only engineer dedicated to hardware in a 10-person team, so I led the redesign end to end and reported
directly to the founders.

## What I did

- **Redesigned the product in Fusion 360**, validating each iteration with 3D printed prototypes.
- **Removed parts** that testing showed were unnecessary, and reselected the body materials and internal components.
- **Optimised the form factor** for faster assembly and simpler installation.
- **Ran a component testing programme:** wrote specifications, sourced suppliers and samples, set acceptance criteria,
  built test rigs, wrote the control code, analysed the data and presented recommendations to the founders.
- **Built the supply chain** across China (pump and components), India (vacuum formed cover), Poland (pipework) and
  the UK (sheet metal), from RFQs and design packages through to managing production deadlines. I visited China to
  work with the pump supplier.

## Result

- Unit cost reduced from **£1,072 to £691 (−36%)**, turning the product profitable.
- **Assembly time halved.**
- Production tooling complete; final product development and inventory ordering under way before assembly.
- A further concept design brings the unit cost to **£289 (−73%)**.
